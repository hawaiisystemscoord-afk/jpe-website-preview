What Is an IWS?

An Individual Wastewater System (IWS) is the technical term used by the Hawaiʻi Department of Health for a septic system or other on-site wastewater treatment system. It is how properties not connected to a municipal sewer treat and dispose of household wastewater.

A typical IWS consists of a septic tank that collects and partially treats solids, followed by a distribution system that spreads liquid effluent into the soil for further treatment. Proper design ensures that wastewater is safely filtered through the soil before reaching groundwater or surface water.

On Hawaiʻi Island, where volcanic geology, diverse rainfall patterns, and variable soil conditions are the norm, a one-size-fits-all approach simply does not work. JPE evaluates each property individually to determine the most appropriate system type and design parameters.

Regulatory Compliance

Hawaiʻi DOH Standards and Permitting

Hawaiʻi's Act 184, passed by the Legislature in 2015, established requirements for wastewater system design for Hawaiʻi properties. Under this law and DOH regulations, certain wastewater system projects must be designed by a licensed professional engineer registered in Hawaiʻi.

Projects Requiring Engineered Design

  • Cesspool-to-septic conversions: Required under Act 184 for properties in designated conversion areas.
  • New septic system design: For residential or commercial construction where no existing IWS is present.
  • System replacements or upgrades: When an existing system is failing or no longer meets current DOH standards.
  • Subdivision wastewater planning: Wastewater system design for subdivided parcels requiring individual or shared IWS.

JPE: DOH-Listed IWS Engineer

JPE Civil & Environmental Engineers is listed on the Hawaiʻi DOH IWS engineer registry. This means JPE is qualified to prepare and seal wastewater system designs that meet state standards. Working with a DOH-listed engineer streamlines the permit application and reduces the risk of rejection or delay.

Design Factors

What JPE Evaluates for Every IWS Design

Soil Percolation Testing

The rate at which soil absorbs water determines system sizing and type. JPE conducts or reviews percolation tests to establish reliable design parameters.

Groundwater Depth

Seasonal and spatial variation in the water table affects setback requirements and the type of treatment system that can be installed.

Lot Size and Topography

Available space, slope, and setback distances from property lines, wells, and streams constrain where a system can be placed.

Volcanic Terrain

Lava rock layers, fractured rock, and steep slopes require specialized engineering approaches that standard methods do not address.

How It Works

The IWS Design Process

1

Site Visit and Records Review

JPE visits the property to assess terrain, soil conditions, access, and existing infrastructure. JPE also reviews TMK records, previous system documentation, and applicable DOH regulations.

2

Soil Analysis and Testing

Soil percolation tests, examination of soil profiles, and groundwater depth assessment provide the data needed to size and design the system.

3

Engineered Plans

A licensed Hawaiʻi PE prepares engineered plans including system layout, sizing calculations, construction details, and all documentation required by DOH. Plans are sealed and ready for permit submission.

4

DOH Permit Submission

JPE prepares and submits the complete permit package to the Hawaiʻi Department of Health, managing all correspondence and responding to reviewer comments until approval is granted.
