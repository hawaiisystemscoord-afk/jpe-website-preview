Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about wastewater systems, engineering services, and working with JPE for Hawaiʻi properties.

What services does JPE provide?

JPE Civil & Environmental Engineers provides licensed civil and wastewater engineering for Hawaiʻi properties. Core services include cesspool conversion to compliant wastewater systems, IWS/septic system design for new construction and replacement, grading and drainage plans, and site civil support for building permits and subdivisions.

JPE also coordinates with builders, architects, and contractors on project-specific engineering needs. Each service follows the same structured five-step process from initial consultation through project closeout.

Do I need a licensed engineer for my project?

For most wastewater system work for Hawaiʻi properties, yes. The Hawaiʻi Department of Health requires engineered design by a licensed Professional Engineer for all new IWS installations and cesspool conversions.

Grading and drainage plans for building permits on the island also typically require a PE stamp. JPE holds Hawaiʻi PE License No. 12035C and is listed on the DOH IWS Engineer registry.

What is a cesspool conversion and why is it required?

A cesspool conversion replaces an existing septic cesspool with a compliant wastewater treatment system, such as an advanced septic system or connection to a community system.

Hawaiʻi Act 184 requires all active cesspools in designated areas to be converted. The Department of Health is designating communities on a phased schedule, and some areas may be required to convert earlier than the full 2050 deadline. JPE guides property owners through the evaluation, design, and permitting process.

How long does a typical project take?

Timelines vary by project type and agency review schedules. A cesspool conversion or IWS design typically takes 6 to 12 weeks from initial contact through permit approval, though this is an estimate and actual timelines depend on your specific property conditions, the complexity of the design, and agency processing times.

JPE provides a realistic timeline estimate during the initial consultation after reviewing your property's specifics.

What information do I need to get started?

The essential information is your TMK (Tax Map Key) number, property address, and the type of project you need. If you do not have your TMK number, your address alone is sufficient and JPE can look it up.

Any existing plans, surveys, or DOH correspondence are helpful but not required to get started. JPE will let you know if additional information is needed during the review.

Does JPE work on projects outside Hawaiʻi Island?

JPE primarily serves Hawaiʻi properties. JPE is familiar with DOH and County requirements across all districts on the island, from North Kohala to Kaʻū.

Projects on other islands may be considered on a case-by-case basis. Contact JPE directly to discuss your specific situation.

How much does a project cost?

Project costs vary significantly based on site conditions, system type, lot accessibility, design complexity, and agency requirements. JPE provides design fee estimates during the initial consultation after reviewing your property's specifics.

Actual construction costs come from licensed installers and are separate from engineering design fees. JPE does not guarantee a specific price before understanding the project scope.

Can JPE help with existing wastewater system issues?

Yes. If your property has an existing cesspool, septic system, or IWS that is not functioning properly or needs to be brought into compliance, JPE can evaluate the situation and recommend appropriate solutions.

This may include system repair, upgrade, or full replacement design. JPE handles the engineering assessment and coordinates with the relevant agencies as needed.
